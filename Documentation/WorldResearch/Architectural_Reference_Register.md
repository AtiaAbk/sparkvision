## 2. Key Architectural Observations Recorded from Field Data

### 1. Curzon Hall Architectural Morphology
* **Historical Baseline**: Foundation laid February 19, 1904 by Viceroy Lord Curzon. Designed during the height of the Bengal partition era as a town hall / library, later handed over to Dhaka College, and incorporated into Dhaka University on July 1, 1921.
* **Mughal & Indo-Saracenic Integration**:
  - Cusped scalloped arches at the central entrance portal and ground-level verandas.
  - Flanking octagonal corner towers measuring 25.2m to finials.
  - Open four-pillared and eight-pillared terrace chhatris with domical cupolas.
  - Overhanging decorative stone eaves (*chhajjas*) supported by carved corbel brackets along the roof perimeter.
  - Symmetrical two-storey brick facades (120m long) with distinct red-oxide coating and dressed white sandstone accents.

### 2. Salimullah Muslim Hall Quadrangle
* **Historical Baseline**: Foundation stone laid August 22, 1929 by Governor Sir Stanley Jackson; inaugurated August 11, 1931. Architecturally planned by the Bengal Public Works Department under provost Sir A. F. Rahman.
* **Fort-Like Massing**:
  - Two-storey quadrangular structure enclosing a 75,000 sq ft central courtyard garden.
  - Southern grand entrance flanked by twin square towers capped with distinctive yellow domes.
  - Four outer corners reinforced with square projecting towers crowned by triple domical cupolas.
  - Pointed Gothic-Islamic arches across common verandas, with flat-lintel window openings for student dormitories.

### 3. Dr. Muhammad Shahidullah Hall (Former Dacca Hall)
* **Historical Baseline**: Built in 1911 as a Hindu hostel for Dhaka College; absorbed on July 1, 1921 as one of DU's three founding residences (Dacca Hall). Renamed in 1969 in honor of legendary linguist Dr. Muhammad Shahidullah.
* **Campus Relationship**:
  - Located on the western bank of the historic perennial Curzon pond.
  - Two-to-three-storey red-brick perimeter blocks arranged in a quadrangular layout enclosing an inner courtyard garden.
  - Dr. Shahidullah's tomb is situated adjacent to the southern gate.

### 4. Muzharul Islam Central Library & Tropical Modernism
* **Historical Baseline**: Designed 1953–1954 as the East Pakistan Public Library; later transferred to Dhaka University.
* **Pioneering Environmental Strategy**:
  - Complete departure from European classical pastiche.
  - Building elevated on a ground-level structural grid of pilotis to maximize breezes and free movement.
  - Deep concrete brise-soleil sun louvers oriented to block intense tropical solar gain while bouncing soft diffuse daylight into reading rooms.
  - Integration of modernist murals by Hamidur Rahman and Novera Ahmed.

### 5. Teacher-Student Centre (TSC) by Constantinos Doxiadis
* **Historical Baseline**: Designed 1961 by Greek urbanist Constantinos A. Doxiadis; funded by the Ford Foundation.
* **Climate Responsive Modernism**:
  - Hexagonal auditorium drum crowned by a sweeping folded-plate cantilevered concrete roof.
  - Ground-floor open pilotis breezeway creating continuous micro-climate cooling across the gathering lawns.
  - Two-storey south cafeteria and activity wing with deep ribbon sun louvers.

### 6. Central Shaheed Minar (Language Movement Memorial)
* **Historical Baseline**: First erected spontaneously Feb 23, 1952; permanent monument designed by Hamidur Rahman and Novera Ahmed in 1957, constructed 1963.
* **Symbolism & Geometry**:
  - 14m central Mother Pillar inclined 15° forward, shielding her martyr children.
  - 5.2m vertical crimson sun disc positioned directly behind the mother pillar.
  - 4 child pillars (8m and 10m heights) flanking the central column.
  - 32m × 22m stepped white marble terrace with black granite border trim.

### 7. Raju Memorial & Aparajeyo Bangla Sculptures
* **Raju Memorial (1997)**:
  - Sculptor: Mahmudul Hasan Shohag.
  - Commemorates student activist Moin Hossain Raju (slain March 13, 1992 during an anti-terrorism march).
  - Circular ring of 8 linked youth figures standing shoulder-to-shoulder on concentric stepped circular dark granite plinths, with central flag bearer reaching 10m height.
* **Aparajeyo Bangla (1979)**:
  - Sculptor: Syed Abdullah Khalid.
  - 11.2m reinforced cast-concrete monument positioned in front of Kala Bhaban.
  - Portrays three figures of the 1971 Liberation War: rural peasant fighter with rifle, female medical volunteer, and student freedom fighter.
