# CAMPUS BAMBOO GROVES & WIND CORRIDORS
**Project**: SparkVision — Level 1 University of Dhaka Digital Twin  
**Aesthetic Benchmark**: *Where Winds Meet* Bamboo Forest Encounters  
**Ecological Reality**: Bengal Golden Bamboo (*Bambusa vulgaris*) & Tulda Bamboo (*Bambusa tulda*) thickets at Dhaka University.

---

## 1. Botanical Character & Growth Architecture
Unlike Chinese or Japanese temperate moso bamboo (*Phyllostachys edulis*), which forms sparse single-stem underground runner colonies, **Bengal Delta Bamboo is a sympodial clumping bamboo**:
* **Culm Structure**: Stems grow in tightly packed, circular clumped thickets containing 20 to 50 interlocked culms springing from a common root crown.
* **Coloration**: Brilliant golden-yellow culms with dark-emerald vertical racing stripes (*Bambusa vulgaris var. striata*), weathering to dull ochre near the base.
* **Dimensions**: Height $12.0\text{ m} - 16.0\text{ m}$; culm diameter $8.0\text{ cm} - 12.0\text{ cm}$.
* **Foliage Density**: Dense tiers of graceful, arching branches producing thousands of slender lanceolate leaves that form an umbrella over the grove interior.

## 2. Gameplay & Atmosphere Design in SparkVision
1. **The Curzon Bamboo Sanctuary (South Perimeter)**:
   - Sited at `(-1500, -2500, 0)` along the southern boundary wall of Curzon grounds.
   - Serves as a secluded, atmospheric meditation and exploration zone.
   - Sunlight filters through the dense yellow-green culms creating moving shaft patterns on the forest floor.
2. **Audio-Driven Atmosphere**:
   - In cross-breezes, the hollow bamboo culms scrape against each other, producing a resonant wooden clicking and deep creaking sound (*Ghashi Shabda*).
   - Wind rushing through dry bamboo leaves generates a soft, high-frequency hiss that masks distant urban traffic noise.
3. **Wind Corridors Across Campus**:
   - Predominant wind vectors follow the North-South and South-West seasonal monsoon corridors.
   - Wind funnels through the Fuller Road avenue and across the Mall field, accelerating as it strikes the Curzon bamboo buffers and causing high-amplitude culm bending.
