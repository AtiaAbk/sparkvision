## 2. Chronological & Structural Evolution
* **1904 (Feb 19)**: Foundation stone laid by Lord Curzon as a combined Town Hall and College Library.
* **1905–1911**: Following the First Partition of Bengal, the building was expanded and repurposed as the administrative centerpiece for the Province of Eastern Bengal and Assam.
* **1911–1921**: Assigned to Dhaka College upon annulment of the partition.
* **1921 (July 1)**: Formally incorporated as the founding science campus for the University of Dhaka, housing the Departments of Physics and Chemistry. Satyendra Nath Bose conducted his historic work here on Bose-Einstein statistics.
* **1948 (March)**: Central Hall was the historic site where Dhaka University students spontaneously stood up and proclaimed "No! No!" to Mohammad Ali Jinnah's declaration that Urdu alone would be the state language, igniting the Bengali Language Movement.

## 4. Key Architectural Signatures
1. **The Double-Tiered Colonnaded Veranda**: Deep ($2.6\text{ m}$) shaded verandas run continuously across the south and north facades, protecting interior halls from tropical monsoon rain and extreme solar heat.
2. **Arched Stylistic Duality**:
   - **Ground Storey**: Broad, load-bearing Moorish horseshoe arches springing from sturdy rectangular/octagonal brick masonry piers.
   - **Upper Storey**: Delicate multi-foil cusped arches (7-lobed and 9-lobed) springing from paired slender octagonal colonnettes.
3. **Projecting Chhajjas & Corbel Brackets**: Pronounced red terracotta drip-eaves projecting $1.2\text{ m}$ outwards at a $35^\circ$ angle, supported by sculpted stepped corbel brackets every $1.0\text{ m}$.
4. **Rajput Chhatris**: Octagonal domed pavilions featuring 8 slender carved stone columns supporting a smooth hemisphere dome surmounted by an inverted lotus base and slender brass finial.
5. **Red Oxide Terracotta & Chuna-Surki Mortar**: Distinctive dark crimson/red-oxide brick masonry joined with lime-surki mortar, accented with white plaster decorative bands and carved jali lattice screens.
