## 1. Architectural Truth Regarding the Curzon Roofline
A recurring error in amateur 3D depictions of Curzon Hall is the inclusion of a gigantic central dome (similar to the Taj Mahal or Kolkata's Victoria Memorial). 

**Historical & Structural Fact**: Curzon Hall was intentionally designed with a **flat terrace roof** intended for evening promenades, astronomical observations, and weather stations. The only domical features are the **Rajput-style chhatri kiosks** crowning the four corner towers and the twin kiosks flanking the central portal.

### Components of the Roof Silhouette:
1. **Flat Accessible Terrace**:
   - High-performance lime-terrace (*lime concrete*) waterproofing layer with red clay flat tiles.
   - Designed to withstand heavy Bengal monsoon downpours ($2,000\text{ mm}$ annual rainfall).
2. **Stepped Merlon Crenellations (Battlements)**:
   - Run continuously along the entire perimeter parapet wall ($1.2\text{ m}$ high).
   - Each merlon is $60\text{ cm}$ wide, $50\text{ cm}$ high above the coping, featuring a 3-tier stepped pyramid peak with pointed stone caps.
   - Spacing (crenel gap): $40\text{ cm}$ between adjacent merlons, providing defensive sightlines and aesthetic rhythm.
3. **Corner Tower Chhatris ($25.2\text{ m}$ Peak)**:
   - Symmetrically anchors both ends of the building against the sky.
   - Hemispherical plaster dome with fluted ribs.
   - Surmounted by an inverted lotus base (*padma*) and a three-tiered brass finial (*kalasa*).
4. **Central Portal Pediment & Twin Kiosks ($23.4\text{ m}$ Peak)**:
   - Breaks the horizontal roofline in the center with a stepped Dutch-Mughal pediment containing a central clock face.
   - Flanked by paired slender octagonal chhatris that mirror the corner towers on a smaller scale ($4.6\text{ m}$ kiosk height).
5. **Gargoyles / Drainage Spouts (*Prastara Nahali*)**:
   - Projecting stone scuppers extending $80\text{ cm}$ beyond the parapet to discharge rainwater clear of the facade walls.
