# CURZON HALL ARCHITECTURAL ANALYSIS & INDO-SARACENIC COMPOSITION
**Project**: SparkVision — Level 1 University of Dhaka Digital Twin  
**Architectural Style**: Anglo-Mughal Indo-Saracenic Revival  
**Comparative Precedents**: Victoria Memorial (Kolkata), Mayo College (Ajmer), High Court (Chennai).

---

## 1. Architectural Philosophy & Context
Curzon Hall represents the zenith of the late 19th / early 20th-century British Indian Public Works Department (PWD) Indo-Saracenic movement. Unlike purely classical European colonial edifices (such as Old High Court or Government House in Kolkata), Curzon Hall deliberately incorporated traditional Islamic, Mughal, and regional Bengali architectural vocabulary to establish visual legitimacy and harmonize with the historic sultanate and Mughal heritage of Dhaka (Jahangirnagar).

The synthesis is evident across three distinct design layers:
1. **Classical European Planning**: Rigid bilateral axial symmetry, tripartite horizontal composition (podium/plinth, piano nobile/veranda, entablature/parapet), and modular structural grid planning.
2. **Imperial Mughal Monumentality**: Stepped monumental entrance portals (reminiscent of the Buland Darwaza at Fatehpur Sikri), cusped arches, chhatri pavilions, bracketed eaves (*chhajja*), and pierced lattice work (*jali*).
3. **Regional Bengal Adaptation**: Deep verandas providing shaded passive cooling against monsoon squalls and humid subtropical heat, coupled with extensive use of deep red terracotta brick masonry native to the Bengal delta.

---

## 2. Structural Bay System & Rhythm
The South Facade is organized into a strictly calculated rhythm:
$$\text{Facade Layout} = \text{Tower (W)} + 6 \times \text{Bay (W)} + \text{Central Portal} + 6 \times \text{Bay (E)} + \text{Tower (E)}$$

* **Bay Spacing**: Exactly $8.0\text{ m}$ center-to-center. Each bay features two structural arch openings ($4.0\text{ m}$ each) separated by a central pilaster/column.
* **Vertical Storey Distribution**:
  - **Plinth / Podium**: Raised $0.8\text{ m}$ above ground level with a 5-step red brick monumental stairway at the central entrance.
  - **Ground Floor**: $6.4\text{ m}$ floor-to-ceiling. Characterized by heavy load-bearing horseshoe arches with $0.85\text{ m}$ thick masonry piers.
  - **Intermediate Belt / Chhajja**: $0.6\text{ m}$ projecting string course and drip-eave with decorative brick dentils.
  - **First Floor (Piano Nobile)**: $6.2\text{ m}$ floor-to-ceiling. Features delicate multi-cusped scalloped arches springing from paired octagonal columns, backed by shaded gallery verandas.
  - **Roof Parapet**: $1.2\text{ m}$ height, adorned with continuous stepped pointed merlons.
  - **Total Eaves Height**: $14.2\text{ m}$ from ground datum.

## 3. Typology of Arches
Curzon Hall employs two distinct arch geometries that define its visual rhythm:

### A. Ground Floor: Moorish Horseshoe Arch
* **Profile**: An ultra-semicircular arch where the arch ring extends below the horizontal diameter line before terminating at the abacus, creating the classic horseshoe constriction.
* **Masonry Dressing**: Five concentric rings of header and stretcher brickwork terminating in a pronounced white plaster keystones and impost moldings.
* **Structural Purpose**: Massive load-bearing integrity supporting the upper veranda and roof load.

### B. First Floor: Mughal Multi-Cusped / Scalloped Arch
* **Profile**: A pointed arch formed by intersecting circular arcs (scallops) creating 7 distinct cusps (lobes) on standard bays and 9 cusps on the central portal arch.
* **Foliation & Tracery**: Decorated with recessed white lime-plaster intrados mouldings and spandrel medallions (*chandras* or floral rosettes).
* **Structural Purpose**: Open arcading providing maximum ventilation and aesthetic lightness.

---

## 4. The *Chhajja* (Projecting Overhanging Eaves)
A defining characteristic of Curzon Hall that distinguishes it from neoclassical British colonial architecture is the *chhajja*:
* **Cantilever**: Projects $1.2\text{ m}$ horizontally outwards from the facade plane at a downward angle of approximately $35^\circ$.
* **Corbel Brackets**: Carved stone/brick corbels placed at regular $1.0\text{ m}$ intervals along the entire facade. Each bracket features a three-stepped S-curve profile with floral scrollwork carvings.
* **Rain Protection**: Effectively prevents tropical monsoon rains from penetrating the verandas while casting deep, dramatic shadows across the red-brick facade.

---

## 5. Rajput-Mughal Chhatris (Rooftop Kiosks)
Curzon Hall features six authentic chhatri kiosks:
* **Two Flanking Corner Towers**: Sited atop the $25.2\text{ m}$ octagonal towers.
* **Two Central Parapet Kiosks**: Sited symmetrically flanking the upper central portal clock pediment.
* **Two Courtyard Pavilions**: Sited on the northern courtyard corner projections.
* **Anatomy of a Curzon Chhatri**:
  1. *Plinth*: Octagonal stone base with molded plinth capping.
  2. *Columns*: 8 slender octagonal columns with bulbous lotus capitals.
  3. *Lintel / Chhajja*: Projecting octagonal drip-eave with mini-brackets.
  4. *Dome*: Smooth hemispherical masonry dome clad in painted lime plaster.
  5. *Finial*: Inverted lotus base (*padma*) surmounted by a brass water-pot finial (*kalasa*).
